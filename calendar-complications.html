Calendar complications explained

From the humble date window to the perpetual calendar, here’s how watches keep track of days, months, years and moons.

BasicsHow

How a date mechanism works

Once a day, around midnight, a wheel linked to the hour hand nudges a ring printed with the numbers 1 to 31. In a day-date watch, a second wheel turns a disc printed with the days of the week.

Some watches change the date slowly over a few hours; others snap over instantly at midnight.

Related displays

Moon phase

A disc with two moons turns behind an aperture, completing a cycle in about 29.5 days.

Big date

Two separate discs show the date in larger digits for better legibility.

Pointer date

A central hand with a crescent tip points to the date on a printed scale around the dial.

MoonPhase

Reading a moon phase display

  • The moon appears from the left as it waxes.
  • It sits centred in the aperture at full moon.
  • It slides away to the right as it wanes.
  • Set it using a full-moon date as your reference.
